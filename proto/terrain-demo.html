What a chamber run actually looks like

No arena and no walls. A generated chain of chambers, each with a node field and a violet exit arrow spanning its whole far side — so you don’t aim at the arrow, you aim at where on it you cross. The camera follows and turns so gravity stays screen‑down.

1 · No re-orientation beat

Crossing into a new chamber, can you read the node field and commit — or is there a beat of “wait, where am I”?

2 · Comfortable at rate

Run a few minutes. Try the ease at 0 ms and 500 ms. Does the turning stay comfortable?

3 · A bad crossing is your fault

Cross the arrow near an edge and the next chamber starts ugly. Does that read as your mistake, or as the terrain’s?

What changed from the arena demo: the arrow now spans the chamber’s full width, so you cross one nearly every time — “missing” means crossing badly, not sailing past a small bar. Death is core contact or leaving the chamber sideways; there are no walls to hit. Chambers stream in ahead of you and are generated from the seed shown above.

Still a throwaway: JavaScript, so it can be iterated in seconds. The force law, the integrator and the slice 1 tuned values are ported faithfully; the real work is Python and none of this ships.
